How it works

Three rules run through every line of this desk.

1 · Computed vs. argued

Every number you see — the analog distribution, price, basis, funding, open interest, session state, coverage, Brier — comes from exchange data, the perp's own archive, the clock, or arithmetic. The model interprets and argues; it never produces a figure. Each row is labelled.

2 · Citation or it's struck

Bull, Bear and Judge may cite only rows in the evidence table, by id. A claim with no citation, or citing a row that came back empty or errored, is struck in code before the Judge reads it.

3 · Missing evidence lowers confidence

Coverage is counted. A verdict on 6 of 12 rows cannot be more than 50% sure — and the desk says so rather than filling the gap with a guess. It is allowed to abstain, and abstentions are scored.

One research task, end to end

  1. Read — a deterministic reader matches any word in your thesis against the live rToken universe and infers direction and horizon. A model refines it; if the thesis can't be judged as written, the desk asks one question and spends nothing else.
  2. Analogs — the comparable windows in the perp's own hourly archive (overnight, weekend, earnings, session) and what it did in each: hit rate, median, p10/p90, worst against, the cash gap each contained, the perp's residual at the cash open, funding to hold. The Laplace-smoothed hit rate is a base-rate forecast.
  3. Evidence — perp, cash, session, gap, headlines, SEC filings, the earnings calendar, prediction markets, a second reading of the perp, technicals. Each bounded in time and marked ok / empty / error.
  4. Is there anything to argue? No perp listed, too little evidence, or markets in agreement with nothing contested → the desk says so and stops. The debate is skipped by rule, not by a model.
  5. Debate — two rounds, four turns, strict JSON, citations validated in code.
  6. Judge — sampled three times, median reported, probability capped to 10–90%, confidence capped by coverage, may abstain.
  7. Ledger & scoreboard — hash-chained before you see it, graded when the horizon elapses, Brier scored against the base rate.

What this is not, and known limits

  • Not a trading bot and never an order: read-only market data, no Trade permission, no Agentic Account. The trader decides.
  • Analog counts follow listing dates — NVDA and TSLA perps listed 2025-08-19 (five earnings prints); MU listed February 2026 (two). The panel always says how many, and from when.
  • The base rate is a base rate: the perp's own history for this window type. It knows nothing about today's news — which is exactly why it's the benchmark the judge has to beat.
  • Exchange holidays are not modelled: on a US holiday the session flag reads “open” during regular hours.
  • The judge's verdict is lint-clean (no buy/sell/long/short). The analysts' transcript is quoted argument and may echo the words in your thesis.
